# Pocket Architect — Image Generation Prompts

This file is the human-readable companion to [`image-gen-prompt.json`](image-gen-prompt.json). It contains one prompt for each of the 44 constraint values.

## Shared style contract

Apply this direction to every prompt:

> Square 1:1 image, preferably 1024 × 1024. Pure black ink on a plain white background. Simple hand-drawn architectural sketch, slightly human and imperfect, with minimal detail, generous negative space, centered composition, and no frame. The concept must be recognizable at thumbnail size but intentionally incomplete. A restrained three-quarter view is allowed for building values; perspective values should read as clean architectural diagrams.

Global negative prompt:

> No color, gray, grayscale shading, gradients, shadows, realistic lighting, photorealism, architectural visualization, finished concept art, detailed floor plans, construction documents, realistic materials, excessive texture, hatching, watercolor, people, furniture, cars, unnecessary plants, text, labels, arrows, dimensions, logos, borders, frames, registration marks, collage layouts, multiple alternative views, or a fully designed final building.

## Building prompts

### 01 — Garden shed

Create a square black-and-white visual cue for a **Garden shed**. Show a small simple shed as a compact block in a restrained three-quarter view, with one clear pitched roof, a plain door, and at most one tiny functional detail. Use a few confident black architectural ink lines on white, centered with generous empty space. Keep it a simple hand-drawn sketch, recognizable in two seconds and open enough for the user to draw their own version.

### 02 — Bus stop shelter

Create a square black-and-white visual cue for a **Bus stop shelter**. Show a minimal shelter as a thin open frame with two or three slender supports and one simple canopy, with no bus or street scene. Use confident black architectural ink lines on white, centered with generous empty space. Keep it human and slightly imperfect, like a quick hand-drawn architectural sketch rather than a finished design.

### 03 — Treehouse

Create a square black-and-white visual cue for a **Treehouse**. Show one compact elevated room nestled against a single simple tree trunk, with a small platform and one ladder hint. Use only the marks needed to show that the room is supported by the tree. Draw it as a centered, slightly imperfect black architectural ink sketch on white with generous empty space.

### 04 — Row house (terrace)

Create a square black-and-white visual cue for a **Row house (terrace)**. Show two or three adjoining narrow house bays as a repeated street-facing mass, with one repeated roof rhythm and a few simple doors. Use sparse black architectural ink lines, centered on white with generous empty space. Keep the repetition recognizable but do not draw a detailed facade or finished streetscape.

### 05 — Beach hut

Create a square black-and-white visual cue for a **Beach hut**. Show a tiny raised hut with a simple box body, a lightweight roof, and two short support legs. Suggest the beach-hut character through the elevated compact form only, without drawing a beach or ocean. Use a centered, hand-drawn black ink sketch on white with very few lines and generous negative space.

### 06 — Library

Create a square black-and-white visual cue for a **Library**. Show a calm, substantial rectangular public building mass with two or three stacked horizontal volumes and one clearly marked entrance. Use simple black architectural ink contours on white, with no books, people, or detailed windows. The result should be a quiet, centered hand-drawn massing sketch with generous empty space.

### 07 — Art museum

Create a square black-and-white visual cue for an **Art museum**. Show an abstract public building with one bold gallery volume and a simple recessed entry, using a slightly sculptural but very sparse massing outline. ### 08 — Hillside cabin

Create a square black-and-white visual cue for a **Hillside cabin**. Show a small cabin stepping down a single diagonal slope, with a simple pitched roof and two offset floor levels. Use only a few black architectural ink lines to show the cabin adapting to the hill. Keep the centered sketch sparse, human, white-backed, and intentionally unfinished.

### 09 — Greenhouse

Create a square black-and-white visual cue for a **Greenhouse**. Show a light rectangular greenhouse frame with a simple pitched or curved roof and a few visible structural ribs. Use sparse black ink lines on white, centered with generous empty space. Do not fill it with plants or render the glass; communicate the delicate frame and transparent volume only.

### 10 — Lakeside villa

Create a square black-and-white visual cue for a **Lakeside villa**. Show a low, elongated villa with a broad roof plane and one open-facing side, using a restrained three-quarter massing view. Use only a few black architectural ink lines on white; do not draw a lake, furniture, or luxury details. Keep the simple villa centered and open for the user's own interpretation.

### 11 — Observatory

Use confident but sparse black architectural ink lines on white, centered with generous empty space. Avoid telescopes, stars, landscape, and technical detail; show only the base-and-dome silhouette.

### 12 — Train station

Create a square black-and-white visual cue for a **Train station**. Show a long simple station hall with one clear large-span canopy and a short platform edge, but no train or people. Use a few centered black architectural ink lines on white with generous negative space. Make the long-span structure readable without turning it into a detailed transportation scene.

### 13 — Cathedral

## Placement prompts

### 17 — Flat open field

Create a square black-and-white visual cue for **Flat open field** placement. Show a generic small building block resting on one long, perfectly straight ground line with almost no surrounding marks. Use sparse black hand-drawn architectural ink on white, centered with abundant empty space. The relationship between the isolated building and the flat horizon should be the only idea.

### 18 — Suburban cul-de-sac

Create a square black-and-white visual cue for **Suburban cul-de-sac** placement. Show a simple building block at the end of a curved dead-end street, with only two or three short driveway or curb lines to establish the setting. Use minimal black architectural ink on white, centered with generous empty space. Avoid houses, cars, people, and a detailed neighborhood.

### 19 — Wooded clearing

Create a square black-and-white visual cue for **Wooded clearing** placement. Show one simple building block surrounded by three or four tall, widely spaced tree trunks, leaving a clear open gap around the building. Use sparse black ink lines on white with generous negative space. Keep the trees as simple vertical marks, not a forest illustration.

### 20 — Quiet residential street

Create a square black-and-white visual cue for **Quiet residential street** placement. Show one simple building set along a straight street edge, with two low neighboring facade silhouettes behind it to establish scale. Use a few black architectural ink lines on white, centered and sparse. Do not draw cars, people, signs, or a detailed neighborhood; show only the calm street relationship.

### 21 — Corner lot

Create a square black-and-white visual cue for **Corner lot** placement. Show one simple building block meeting two straight, perpendicular street or ground edges. Use only the building, the two intersecting edges, and a small amount of open space. Render it as a centered black architectural ink sketch on white, with the two public fronts clearly readable and no streetscape detail.

### 22 — Dense urban alley

Create a square black-and-white visual cue for **Dense urban alley** placement. Show a narrow building volume squeezed between two tall, close parallel wall edges, leaving only a thin strip of sky above. Use sparse black architectural ink on white, centered with generous empty space beyond the alley marks. Avoid windows, graffiti, people, and detailed city texture.

### 23 — Rooftop garden

Show a simple small pavilion or building block sitting on a flat roof slab, with only two or three simplified planter or plant marks around it. ### 24 — Riverbank

Create a square black-and-white visual cue for **Riverbank** placement. Show a simple building block beside one clear, gently irregular water edge, with a small gap or bank between building and water. Use minimal black ink lines on white, centered with generous negative space. Avoid reflections, boats, vegetation, and landscape detail; preserve the building-to-water relationship.

### 25 — Desert plain

Show one stark simple building block on a long flat horizon, with one or two sparse ground marks suggesting an empty arid expanse. ### 26 — Reclaimed marsh

Use sparse black architectural ink on white, centered with generous empty space. Keep the marsh legible through elevation and water, not through a detailed wetland scene.

### 27 — Town square

Create a square black-and-white visual cue for **Town square** placement. Show one simple building facade framing one side of an open square, with two short surrounding facade edges forming a public void. Use minimal black architectural ink on white, centered and spacious. ### 28 — Bridge over a road

## Perspective prompts

### 33 — Elevation

Create a square black-and-white visual cue for the **Elevation** perspective. Draw a generic architectural block as a flat, straight-on front face with a simple ground line and absolutely no receding depth lines. Use sparse, confident black diagrammatic ink on white, centered with generous empty space. Communicate a measured facade view, not a finished building.

### 34 — Plan (top-down)

Create a square black-and-white visual cue for the **Plan (top-down)** perspective. Draw a simple building footprint viewed directly from above, using one outer contour and two or three interior room or circulation lines. Use minimal black architectural diagram ink on white, centered with generous empty space. Keep it schematic and incomplete; do not create a detailed floor plan or add furniture, dimensions, or labels.

### 35 — One-point perspective

Create a square black-and-white visual cue for **One-point perspective**. Draw a generic box-like architectural volume with its receding horizontal edges converging toward one implied vanishing point, while verticals remain vertical. Use only a few black construction lines on white, centered with generous empty space. Do not render a room, street, or finished building; make the single convergence system the concept.

### 36 — Two-point perspective

Create a square black-and-white visual cue for **Two-point perspective**. Draw a generic architectural corner volume with left and right horizontal edges receding toward two implied vanishing points, while verticals remain straight. Use sparse black hand-drawn diagram lines on white, centered and spacious. Keep the block generic and unfinished; the two-direction convergence must carry the image.

### 37 — Section (cut-through)

Create a square black-and-white visual cue for the **Section (cut-through)** perspective. Draw a simple building block sliced open vertically, exposing one floor plate and the empty volume above and below it. Use a bold cut edge plus a few light architectural ink lines on white, centered with generous empty space. Do not add furniture, materials, annotations, or a detailed plan; show the act of cutting through space.

### 38 — Axonometric

Create a square black-and-white visual cue for the **Axonometric** perspective. Draw a simple block and footprint lifted upward with parallel receding edges and no vanishing points. Use a few confident black architectural diagram lines on white, centered with generous empty space. Keep all edges clean but slightly hand-drawn, and do not turn the generic block into a finished building.

### 39 — Isometric

Create a square black-and-white visual cue for the **Isometric** perspective. Draw a compact generic architectural block with three clearly balanced axes and equal-looking diagonal directions. Use sparse black ink lines on a plain white square, centered with generous empty space. Keep it diagrammatic and incomplete, with no textures, materials, labels, or detailed building design.

### 40 — Three-point perspective

Create a square black-and-white visual cue for **Three-point perspective**. Draw a simple tall block whose horizontal edges recede in two directions and whose vertical edges subtly converge toward a third implied point above or below. Use minimal black architectural construction lines on white, centered with generous empty space. The three convergence directions should be clear without creating a cinematic tower scene.

### 41 — Worm's-eye view

Create a square black-and-white visual cue for the **Worm's-eye view** perspective. Draw the underside and rising faces of a generic architectural block as if viewed sharply upward from near the ground. Use a few bold black architectural lines on white, centered with generous empty space. Keep the view simple and diagrammatic; avoid a dramatic sky, city, facade detail, or finished building.

### 42 — Bird's-eye / aerial

Create a square black-and-white visual cue for the **Bird's-eye / aerial** perspective. Draw a generic building block from a steep high viewpoint so the roof and footprint are both visible, with a small ground outline beneath. Use sparse black architectural ink on white, centered with generous empty space. Do not add a city, landscape, map symbols, or realistic aerial atmosphere; show only the elevated view relationship.

### 43 — Exploded axon

Create a square black-and-white visual cue for the **Exploded axon** perspective. Draw a simple axonometric building separated into two or three vertically aligned layers with clear gaps between them. Use minimal black architectural ink lines on white, centered with generous empty space. Keep each layer generic and schematic; do not add labels, arrows, assembly detail, materials, or a fully designed building.

### 44 — Sectional perspective

Create a square black-and-white visual cue for the **Sectional perspective** perspective. Draw a generic architectural volume cut open on one side, showing a simple interior floor and spatial depth in perspective. Use a few bold cut edges and sparse black ink lines on white, centered with generous empty space. Keep the interior abstract and incomplete; do not draw furniture, materials, or a finished room.

## Acceptance checklist

- Exactly 44 prompts exist: 16 building, 16 placement, and 12 perspective.
- Every prompt names exactly one source constraint value.
- Every image is specified as square, black-and-white, centered, sparse, and unframed.
- Building prompts use simple object sketches; placement prompts show a building/site relationship; perspective prompts show a drawing system.
- No prompt asks for a finished drawing, photorealism, color, text, labels, or decorative detail.
- Each prompt is distinct enough to generate a recognizable visual cue for its value while sharing the same system.
